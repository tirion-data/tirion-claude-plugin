# Prospect scoring rubric

A simple, explained rubric. It is a convention for ordering a list, not a statistical model. Adjust the thresholds to the organization's gift sizes, and tell the user what you adjusted.

Score each dimension 0 to 3. Keep the scores in separate columns. Write one line of evidence, with source and date, for every score above 0.

## Capacity (C)

Capacity is the estimated gift an individual could make over about five years, from public wealth evidence. Set the bands against the organization's major-gift threshold (MG). Example for MG = $100K:

| Score | Meaning | Example band (MG = $100K) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Well above the top gifts the organization seeks | $1M+ |
| 2 | Major-gift capacity | $250K to $999K |
| 1 | At or near the major-gift threshold | $100K to $249K |
| 0 | Below threshold, or no evidence | under $100K, or unknown |

Capacity ladder used by Tirion (five-year capacity): A1 $100M+, A2 $50-99M, A3 $25-49M, A4 $10-24M, A5 $5-9.9M, B1 $2.5-4.9M, B2 $1-2.4M, C1 $500-999K, C2 $250-499K, C3 $100-249K, D1 $50-99K, D2 $25-49K, D3 $10-24K, D4 under $10K. Quote the band, not a point figure.

Rules of thumb:
- One kind of evidence (for example, one house) gives a floor with low confidence. Say so.
- The wealthiest people often show the least in public records; private companies and trusts are hidden. A senior role with little visible wealth is a reason to research further, not a reason to score low.
- Unknown is 0 with a note "unknown", not a judgement that capacity is low.

## Inclination (I): do they give?

| Score | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 3 | Documented major gifts to peer institutions; officer or substantial contributor of a family foundation; several nonprofit boards |
| 2 | A nonprofit board seat; public donor-roll listings at mid levels; trustee of a foundation |
| 1 | Political giving only; a single small public gift; volunteer role |
| 0 | No evidence found |

Political giving is a weak signal of discretionary money and willingness to give. Do not score it above 1.

## Affinity (A): connection to this organization

| Score | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 3 | Current donor at a meaningful level, board or committee member, or alum with active engagement |
| 2 | Past donor, alum, event attendee, parent of an alum, grantee relationship with their foundation |
| 1 | Inferred interest: their foundation funds this cause; a peer's board; a stated public interest in the mission |
| 0 | No evidence |

Mark inferred affinity (score 1) as inferred, with its basis.

## Timing (T): a reason to act now

| Score | Evidence (with dates) |
|---|---|
| 3 | Liquidity event in the last 12 months: company sale closed, IPO lock-up expired, large Form 4 sales, sale of a major property |
| 2 | Career change (retirement, new CEO role), a milestone (reunion year, anniversary), campaign launch in their area of interest |
| 1 | Event more than 12 months ago, or expected soon (announced deal not yet closed) |
| 0 | None known |

For a liquidity event, the first contact is congratulatory, not an ask.

## Access (X): a warm path

| Score | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 3 | A board member, volunteer or donor with a documented close tie (family, business partner, long board co-service) |
| 2 | A documented shared board, employer or co-ownership with someone in the organization's circle |
| 1 | A plausible path that needs confirming (same club, same small town, same class year) |
| 0 | None known |

## Tiering

A convention that keeps capacity and affinity both necessary:

- **Tier A (see this quarter):** C >= 2 and A >= 2, and (T >= 2 or X >= 2).
- **Tier B (cultivate):** C >= 2 and A >= 1; or C >= 1 and A >= 2 with I >= 2.
- **Tier C (steward or monitor):** everyone else with C >= 1.
- **Research lead:** C >= 2 and A = 0. Not a tier; a list for more research.
- **Not a major-gift prospect now:** C = 0.

Then read the list and override where judgement says so. Write the reason for every override.

## Sanity checks

- Tier A usually holds a small share of the list, sized to what one officer can see in a quarter (often 10 to 25 people for a full-time major-gift officer; a convention, not a rule).
- Practitioners often use a 3:1 or 4:1 ratio of qualified prospects to gifts needed at each level of a gift-range chart. If a campaign needs 5 gifts at a level, look for 15 to 20 prospects rated there.
- The ask is not the capacity. Capacity is the ceiling; the ask is set lower by affinity, inclination and engagement.
