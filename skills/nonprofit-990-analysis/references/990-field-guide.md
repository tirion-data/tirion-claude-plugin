# Form 990, 990-EZ and 990-PF: a Part-by-Part field guide for prospect research

Where to read a return: ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer
(projects.propublica.org/nonprofits) shows e-filed returns with parsed
figures and the full PDF; IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (TEOS,
apps.irs.gov/app/eos) has copies of returns, determination letters and
revocations; Candid (candid.org, formerly GuideStar and Foundation Center)
adds profiles and grants data, some behind a subscription. Cite the form, the
organization, the fiscal year, the part and line: "IRS Form 990-PF, Doe
Family Foundation, FY2023, Supplementary Information line 3".

Part numbers change when the IRS redesigns a form. Find a section by its
title if the number does not match. Returns are usually filed 4.5 to 10.5
months after the fiscal year ends (with extension), so the newest public
return is often one to two years old.

## Form 990 (public charities)

| Section | What it holds | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Header (items A-O) | Name, EIN, address, fiscal year, website, year formed, state of legal domicile | Identity of the organization. The fiscal year for every citation. |
| Part I, Summary | Mission, revenue, expenses, net assets, number of voting board members, employees, volunteers | Size of the organization. Scale of a board seat. |
| Part III, Program Service Accomplishments | Largest programs and their spending | What the organization actually does. |
| Part IV, Checklist of Required Schedules | Yes/no lines that say which schedules are attached | Tells you whether Schedule I, J, L, B exist. |
| Part VI, Governance | Board size, independence, family or business relationships among officers (line 2), policies | Line 2 flags family ties among board members. |
| **Part VII, Section A** | Officers, directors, trustees, key employees and the five highest-paid employees over $100,000: name, title, average hours per week, reportable pay from the organization and related organizations, other pay | The main source for board roles and executive pay. Hours show how involved a trustee is. |
| Part VII, Section B | Five highest-paid independent contractors over $100,000 | Law firms, consultants. Rarely about a person. |
| Part VIII, Statement of Revenue | Contributions, program revenue, investment income | Line 1: contributions and grants received. |
| Part X, Balance Sheet | Assets, liabilities, net assets | Endowment size. |
| Schedule A | Public charity status and public support test | Confirms public charity versus private foundation. |
| **Schedule B, Schedule of Contributors** | Contributors of $5,000 or more (or 2% of contributions, for some charities) | For a public charity the public copy has names and addresses removed. Amounts may still show. You cannot identify donors from it. |
| Schedule D | Endowment funds, donor-advised funds held by the organization (Part I) | A community foundation's DAF count and totals. |
| **Schedule I** | Grants over $5,000 to US organizations and governments: recipient, EIN, amount, purpose | What the charity funds. For a DAF sponsor, grants made from donor funds, without the donor's name. |
| Schedule J | Compensation detail for people over $150,000 (base, bonus, deferred, benefits) | Executive pay breakdown. |
| **Schedule L, Transactions With Interested Persons** | Excess benefit transactions, loans to and from insiders, grants to insiders' family, business transactions with officers, directors and their family or companies | Shows a trustee's business ties to the charity; names family members in business with it. |
| **Schedule O** | Narrative answers to other parts | Governance details, how pay is set, explanations of related-party ties. Read it; facts hide here. |
| Schedule R | Related organizations | Sister foundations, supporting organizations. |

## Form 990-EZ (smaller public charities)

| Section | What it holds | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Part I | Revenue, expenses, net assets | Size. |
| Part IV | Officers, directors, trustees and key employees: title, hours, pay | Board roles. |
| Part V | Other information | Governance answers. |
| Schedule B | Contributors (redacted in public copies for public charities) | As for Form 990. |
| Schedule O | Narrative | As for Form 990. |

There is no Schedule I on a 990-EZ. Grants are summarized, not itemized.

## Form 990-PF (private foundations)

Private foundations file a 990-PF regardless of size. It is the best source
on a family foundation. The labels below use recent numbering; older returns
put the officer list in Part VIII and grants in Part XV.

| Section | What it holds | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Header, item I | Fair market value (FMV) of all assets at year end | Size of the foundation. Use FMV, not book value. |
| **Part I, Analysis of Revenue and Expenses** | Line 1: contributions received. Line 25: contributions, gifts, grants paid. Lines 13-24: operating expenses including officer pay | Line 1 across years shows when the family funded the foundation. A large inflow often follows a liquidity event. Line 25 is the annual giving. |
| Part II, Balance Sheets | Cash, securities (corporate stock, bonds), other investments, land | Holdings of a named company's stock can point to the family's business. |
| Part IV, Capital Gains and Losses | Securities sold during the year | Shows which stock the foundation holds and sold. |
| Part VI-A and VI-B, Statements Regarding Activities | Yes/no questions on self-dealing and other restricted acts; lists of substantial contributors may be attached | Explains unusual arrangements. |
| **Information About Officers, Directors, Trustees** (Part VII on recent returns) | Names, titles, hours, pay, benefits | The decision-makers. Adult family members on the board are the next generation. |
| Minimum Investment Return and Distributable Amount (Parts IX and X on recent returns) | The 5% calculation | Shows the required payout. |
| Qualifying Distributions (Part XI on recent returns) | What was actually paid out for charity | Compare to the distributable amount. |
| **Supplementary Information, line 3** (Part XIV on recent returns) | Grants and contributions paid during the year, and approved for future payment: recipient name and address, relationship to any foundation manager, recipient status, purpose, amount | The grant list. Sort by amount and group by purpose and location. "Approved for future payment" shows pledges. |
| Supplementary Information, line 2 | Application instructions, or a checkbox that the foundation "only makes contributions to preselected charitable organizations" | Whether it accepts proposals. |
| **Schedule B** | Contributors of $5,000 or more with names, addresses and amounts | Public for private foundations. Names the family members who funded it, and the year. |

### The 5% payout rule

A private non-operating foundation must make qualifying distributions of
about 5% of the average FMV of its non-charitable-use assets each year, or
pay an excise tax (Internal Revenue Code section 4942). Excess distributions
can carry forward five years, and a shortfall can be made up by the end of
the next year. So read two or three years before calling a foundation
"stingy" or "generous".

Reading payout (grants paid divided by FMV assets):
- About 5%: meets the minimum; typical for a perpetual foundation.
- Clearly above 5%: active giving, a spend-down, or a pledge being paid.
- A sudden jump in assets with little change in grants: a new gift in,
  often after a liquidity event. Check Part I line 1 and Schedule B.

## What a family foundation tells you

- **Capacity floor.** The family once had at least the amount they put in
  (sum Part I line 1 over the years). Those assets are no longer theirs; the
  floor is about past wealth.
- **Interests.** Grant purposes by dollar share. Report as "funds" not
  "believes".
- **Geography.** Where the recipients are.
- **Decision-makers.** The trustees, and how involved each is (hours).
- **Openness.** Preselected-only means relationship access, not a proposal.

## Donor-advised funds

A DAF is an account at a sponsoring charity. The sponsor owns the assets and
files the 990. Grants appear on the sponsor's Schedule I under the sponsor's
name. The advising donor is not named anywhere on the return. Evidence that
links a person to a DAF comes from outside the 990: a donor roll listing "the
Doe Family Fund at <sponsor>", a grant acknowledgment, an annual report, or
a news story. Cite that source.

## Candid, ProPublica and TEOS: which for what

| Need | Best source |
|---|---|
| Latest return PDF and parsed financials | ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer |
| Confirm exempt status, revocation, 990-N filers | IRS TEOS |
| Grants data across many funders, foundation profiles | Candid (may need a subscription) |
| Search by a person's name inside returns | ProPublica full-text search of e-filed returns |
