# Prohibited categories and sensitive records

## Prohibited: never in a document

| Category | Examples | Why | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private contact details | Personal cell, personal email, home phone, private social handles | Donor privacy; the Donor Bill of Rights expects professional relationships | Use the organization's own records, a business line, or a warm introduction |
| Minors | A child's name, school, sports team, photo | Children cannot consent; no gift decision needs it | Omit. State an adult child's public role if relevant |
| Health | Diagnosis, treatment, disability, mental health, "may be ill" | Private; speculation is harmful; HIPAA governs patient data | Report only a public role change with its filing and date |
| Marriage speculation | "Marriage in trouble", affairs, "estranged" | Speculation, not fact | Report a court filing only if it changes capacity (see below) |
| Sexual orientation | Any statement or inference | Private; no bearing on a gift | Omit |
| Religion by inference | "Probably Catholic" from a gift or a name | Inference about belief | State a public role (for example, trustee of a named congregation) as a role |
| Ethnicity or national origin by inference | Inferred from a surname | Inference; discriminatory | Omit |
| Immigration status | Visa type, citizenship questions | Private; no bearing on a gift | Omit |
| Non-public financial records | Bank or brokerage balances, tax returns, credit reports, private-company financials | Not public; credit reports are protected under FCRA | Use public proxies: SEC filings, assessed property, 990 assets. Treat a private company as an upside flag, not a number |
| Trust contents | Beneficiaries and terms of a trust | Not public | The existence of a trust, from a deed or filing, is a public fact |
| Sealed or expunged records | Juvenile records, expunged convictions | Legally closed | Omit |
| Private or login-gated content | Locked social accounts, private groups, paywalled databases scraped without licence | Not public; may breach terms or law | Use public profiles and licensed sources only |

## Methods that are never acceptable

- Pretexting: calling or messaging under a false identity to get information.
- Scraping content behind a login, or breaking a site's terms of use.
- Buying data from a broker that cannot name its sources.
- Asking a prospect's friend, employee or family member for private information under a false purpose.

## Sensitive but public records: the materiality test

Include the record only if (1) it is material to a gift decision, (2) it is cited to the primary record or a reputable report with a date, and (3) it is stated neutrally.

| Record | Usually material when | Usually not material when | How to state it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divorce filing | Recent (within about 3 years) and the couple held significant joint assets; it changes capacity or who controls a family foundation | Decades old and settled; no asset effect | "Divorce filed, [county] Circuit Court, case no. [x], [date]. Terms not public; joint real estate at [address] was transferred by deed on [date]." |
| Civil lawsuit | Large judgment against the prospect; a dispute over the business that is the source of wealth | Routine commercial disputes; small claims | "[Plaintiff] v. [defendant], [court], filed [date]; alleges [one line]; status [open/settled]." |
| Criminal record | Conviction for fraud, financial crime or violence that creates reputational risk for a named gift | Minor offences; arrests without conviction; old and unrelated matters | "Convicted of [offence], [court], [date]; sentence [x]." Arrests without charges stay out |
| Bankruptcy | Recent personal or business bankruptcy that removes assets | Discharged long ago with later rebuilding documented | "Chapter [7/11] petition, U.S. Bankruptcy Court [district], case no. [x], filed [date]." |
| Tax lien | Large and recent; signals distress | Small and released | Cite county recorder and date |
| Regulatory action | SEC or FINRA action, state AG action, professional licence discipline | Resolved minor administrative matters | Cite the agency release with its date |

## Worked examples

- A prospect's spouse filed for divorce last year, and the couple's $6M home was sold. Material: capacity changes. Cite the court docket and the deed. Do not describe the marriage.
- A prospect was sued by a former business partner in 2009 and the case settled. Not material to a 2026 gift unless the dispute concerns the source of wealth. Leave out.
- A prospect's name matches a 2018 securities-fraud conviction. Confirm identity first (middle name, age, city, employer). If it is the same person, include it neutrally and flag it for the gift-acceptance committee. If identity is not confirmed, write "a person of the same name was convicted of ...; identity not confirmed" only in the vetting memo, never in a cultivation briefing.

## Laws named, not interpreted

- GDPR and UK GDPR (EU and UK residents). Article 14 covers data not collected from the person.
- CCPA/CPRA (California residents) and similar laws in other US states.
- FCRA (consumer credit reports), HIPAA (protected health information), FERPA (student records).
- CAN-SPAM and state telemarketing rules govern outreach, not research, but apply to anything built from a prospect list.

Tell the user to ask counsel or their data-protection officer about applicability.
