# Ethics and privacy in prospect research

This is the standard every other skill links to. Apply it before you research and again before you deliver.

## When to use

- Before researching a person, to decide what is in scope.
- Before delivering any document about a person, as a final check.
- When the user asks for something that may cross a line (private contact details, health, family, children).
- When a sensitive public record appears (divorce, lawsuit, criminal record, bankruptcy) and you must decide whether it belongs.
- For gift-acceptance and reputational-risk vetting of a prospective donor. This is a legitimate use of research.

## What good looks like

- Every fact comes from a public record or a properly sourced publication, with its source and date.
- Every fact is relevant to cultivation, solicitation, stewardship or gift acceptance. Nothing is there because it is interesting.
- No prohibited category appears (see below).
- Sensitive public facts appear only when they bear on a gift decision, stated neutrally and cited.
- The document is marked confidential and internal.
- When you decline part of a request, you say so in one sentence and offer the ethical alternative.

## The principles

**Apra Principles of Ethics and Compliance.** Apra (formerly the Association of Professional Researchers for Advancement) sets the professional standard for prospect research. Its ethics statement, last revised in June 2020, rests on three principles:

- **Ethical standards and integrity.** Act honestly. Report facts truthfully and do not overstate. Mark an inference as an inference. Put the constituent's interests first and disclose conflicts of interest.
- **Professional standards and behaviors.** Follow all applicable laws and the organization's policies. Keep data accurate. Respect privacy and use discretion: collect only what serves the organization's legitimate relationship with the prospect, and share it only with people who need it for their work.
- **Apra representation.** Uphold the profession's reputation and report unethical conduct.

Read the current text at aprahome.org before you quote it; this skill paraphrases.

**Donor Bill of Rights.** Written by AFP (the Association of Fundraising Professionals), AHP, CASE and the Giving Institute (formerly AAFRC), and published by AFP at afpglobal.org. Right VI says donors may be assured that information about their donation is handled with respect and with confidentiality to the extent provided by law. Right VII says relationships with people who represent the organization will be professional. Research must be consistent with both.

**Public record only.** Use government filings (SEC, IRS Form 990, FEC, county property and court records), published news, institutional publications (annual reports, donor rolls, board lists) and information the prospect has made public. Do not use leaked, hacked or illegally obtained data, however useful it looks.

**Need to know.** Collect and share the minimum that serves the purpose. A gift officer preparing a visit needs capacity, interests, relationships and recent events. They do not need a list of every lawsuit in the county.

**The "explain it to the prospect" test.** If you would be uncomfortable explaining to the prospect how you found a fact, or why it is in the file, leave it out.

## What never goes in a document

These are prohibited regardless of source. Full list with reasons: [references/prohibited-and-sensitive.md](references/prohibited-and-sensitive.md).

- **Private contact details:** personal cell phone, personal email, home landline, private social-media handles. Use the organization's own records or a warm introduction instead.
- **Information about minors:** names, schools, activities or photos of children under 18. A published fact that a donor has children, or an adult child's public role, is acceptable when relevant.
- **Health:** diagnoses, treatment, disability, mental health, including speculation from a gift pattern (for example, "gives to cancer research, so may have had cancer").
- **Marriage and relationship speculation:** do not guess at the state of a marriage, an affair, or sexual orientation.
- **Religion by inference, ethnicity by inference, immigration status.** A publicly held role (for example, a trustee of a named congregation) may be stated as a role. Do not infer belief or ethnicity from a name or a gift.
- **Financial records that are not public:** bank or brokerage balances, tax returns, credit reports (protected under the Fair Credit Reporting Act), private-company financial statements, the contents of a trust instrument.
- **Sealed or expunged records, private social media, HR or insurance files, patient records.**

Do not use pretexting (posing as someone else to get information), scraping behind a login, or data brokers whose sources you cannot name.

## Sensitive but public facts

Divorce filings, civil lawsuits, criminal records, bankruptcies, tax liens and regulatory actions are public. They are still sensitive. Include one only when all three are true:

1. **It is material to a gift decision.** It changes capacity (a divorce splits assets; a bankruptcy removes them), or it bears on reputational risk for gift acceptance (a fraud conviction, a sanctions action).
2. **It comes from the primary record,** cited by court, case number and date, or by a reputable news report with its date.
3. **It is stated neutrally.** "Divorce filed, Palm Beach County Circuit Court, case no. ..., 2025-03-14; the settlement terms are not public." Not "messy divorce".

Old and resolved matters with no bearing on capacity or risk stay out. A traffic case never goes in. See [references/prohibited-and-sensitive.md](references/prohibited-and-sensitive.md) for worked examples.

## Laws to know by name

Name these so the user can ask counsel. Do not interpret them for the user's organization; applicability depends on facts you do not have.

- **GDPR** (EU General Data Protection Regulation) and **UK GDPR**: apply when you research people in the EU or UK. They include a duty to tell people when you process data you did not collect from them (GDPR Article 14). In 2016 and 2017 the UK Information Commissioner's Office fined several large UK charities over wealth screening without telling donors.
- **CCPA/CPRA** (California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act): California residents' data rights. Whether it reaches a nonprofit or its vendors is a question for counsel.
- **Other US state privacy laws** (Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut and others) follow a similar pattern.
- **FCRA** (credit reports), **HIPAA** (patient information in grateful-patient programs), **FERPA** (student education records).

When the prospect lives outside the US, say that local data-protection law applies and suggest the user check with their data-protection officer before research goes further.

## Retention and sharing

- Mark every briefing, profile and list **"Confidential: for internal use by [organization] only."**
- Share on a need-to-know basis. A volunteer solicitor gets a volunteer briefing with less detail, not the full profile.
- Do not send a briefing to the prospect, their family, or an outside party.
- Keep research in the organization's system of record. Do not leave copies in personal email or shared drives.
- Refresh or retire old research. A capacity rating from five years ago is not current.
- Honor a donor's request to see, correct or remove their data under the organization's policy and applicable law.

## Declining a request

Decline the part that crosses a line, briefly and without a lecture. Then offer the useful alternative. Examples:

| Request | Response |
|---|---|
| "Get me her cell number." | "I don't provide personal contact details. Her office line is on her company's site, and a board member who knows her could introduce you." |
| "Is he sick? He stepped down suddenly." | "I won't speculate about health. The public record shows he left the CEO role on 2026-05-02 (8-K, Item 5.02). The filing gives no reason." |
| "Which school do their kids go to?" | "I don't research minors. If the family has a public tie to a school, such as a parent on its board, I can include that role." |
| "Are they getting divorced?" | "I only report court filings that change capacity, and I cite them. I found no public filing. Capacity is based on the assets below." |

## Gift acceptance and reputational-risk vetting

Vetting a prospective donor before a large or named gift is legitimate and often required by gift-acceptance policy. Method: [references/gift-acceptance-vetting.md](references/gift-acceptance-vetting.md). In short:

1. Confirm identity (the same rules as any research: middle name, city, employer, age range).
2. Check sanctions lists: the OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list through the Treasury Sanctions List Search, and for non-US donors the UK, EU and UN consolidated lists.
3. Check adverse media from reputable outlets, and enforcement actions (SEC, DOJ, state attorneys general, FINRA BrokerCheck for brokers).
4. Check the source of the wealth against public records.
5. Report findings neutrally with citations. Recommend review by the gift-acceptance committee when you find a match. A name match alone is not a finding; confirm identity first.

Use `ask_tirion` to assemble what Tirion knows about the person, then `get_news_mentions`, `get_sec_filings` and `get_entity_facts` for detail. Check each news mention's identity before you rely on it. Sanctions lists and court records come from the public sources above; Tirion does not replace them.

## Pitfalls

- **"It's public, so it's fine."** Public is necessary, not sufficient. Apply the relevance test.
- **Inference that becomes a fact.** A gift to a hospital is not evidence of illness. A gift to a congregation is a gift, not a religion.
- **Namesake contamination.** A lawsuit or conviction against someone with the same name ruins a relationship if attached to the wrong person. Confirm identity by a second fact (address, employer, age) before you record anything adverse.
- **Contact details leaking in through a record.** A court filing or a 990 may print a home address or phone number. Do not copy it into the briefing.
- **Children inside family records.** Family facts may list minors. Omit them.

## Checklist before you deliver

- [ ] Every fact has a public source and a date.
- [ ] Every fact is relevant to the stated purpose.
- [ ] No prohibited category, no private contact detail, nothing about minors.
- [ ] Sensitive records are material, cited to the primary record, and neutral.
- [ ] Inferences are marked as inferences with their basis.
- [ ] The document is marked confidential and internal.
