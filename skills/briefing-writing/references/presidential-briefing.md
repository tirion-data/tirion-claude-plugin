# Template: presidential briefing

For a president, chancellor, dean or board chair before a meeting with one
prospect. Three to six pages. It is the profile a research shop keeps, cut to
what the meeting needs, with a meeting section on top. If the reader wants one
page, use [one-page-briefing.md](one-page-briefing.md) instead.

Two parts:

1. **The meeting section** (first page): logistics, objectives, strategy, talking
   points, handle with care. The officer usually supplies the objectives and the
   ask; research drafts them and marks what came from whom.
2. **The profile** (the rest): research summary, background, family, wealth,
   capacity, giving to us, relationship, contact history, in that order. This is
   the order research shops use, so a reader who knows the format finds each
   fact where they expect it.

```markdown
# Presidential briefing: <Full name>
**Confidential: for internal use by <organization> only**
Prepared <YYYY-MM-DD> by <researcher or team>, with AI assistance. Reviewed by <name>, <date>.
Requested by <officer>. For <reader, title>.

## Meeting
| | |
|---|---|
| Date, time, place | <...> |
| Attending | <our people; their people, including spouse if invited> |
| Purpose | <introduce / cultivate / ask / thank / steward> |
| Prospect manager | <officer> |

## Objectives
1. <e.g., Thank for the 2025 scholarship gift and report on the students it funded>
2. <e.g., Test interest in the new engineering building>
3. <e.g., Agree a follow-up visit by the dean in the spring>

## Strategy
- **Ask (if this meeting is an ask):** <amount>, for <designation>, payable over <years>. Who asks: <name>. How: <in the room / a written proposal to follow>.
- **If not an ask:** what we want them to say or agree to by the end.
- **Research capacity:** <tier label, low to high over 3-5 years, based on <indicator>>. **Officer's expected next gift:** <range and period, or "not yet set">.
- **Timing:** <why now: a liquidity event, a campaign phase, a reunion year, a pledge ending>.

## Talking points
1. <point tied to their interest and our need, with the fact behind it>
2. <...>
3. <...>

## Handle with care
- <a declined ask and why; a recent death; a dispute; a gift to a rival; a request they made that we have not met. Neutral, sourced, or "None known.">

## Research summary
<4-6 sentences: who they are; their relationship with us and the most recent
contact; capacity in the form "Major gift capacity: <low> to <high> (tier), based
on <indicator>"; the officer's expected next gift if set; strongest affinity and
warm path; key unknowns.>

## Identity and education
| | |
|---|---|
| Name (and other names used) | <...> |
| City and state | <...> [n] |
| Education | <institution, degree, year; student activities if public> [n] |
| Spouse or partner | <name; their degree or tie to us if any> [n] |

## Background
<Current role and organization, what it does, size if public; career in a few
lines with years; corporate boards. Cite.>

## Affiliations and honors
- Professional: <...> [n]
- Civic and nonprofit boards: <...> [n]
- Honors: <...> [n]

## Family and household
| Person | Relationship | Public role or tie to us | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

Adults only. No minors, no dates of birth, no children's schools, no
speculation about a marriage. Each link rests on a record.

## Wealth indicators
<By category, each fact with its record and date: real estate (assessed and
estimated market), public-company stock and Form 4 activity, compensation,
private company or partnership signals, family foundation, giving elsewhere,
statements the prospect made to us (labelled self-reported), political giving
as a signal only.>

## Capacity
**Major gift capacity: <low> to <high> (tier), over 3-5 years, based on <indicators>.**
Confidence: <level>, because <reason>. Upside not in the range: <...>.
Unknown: <what, and what would settle it>.
Organization's rating: <officer or overall rating, date>; research rating: <tier>. <Note any gap; do not merge.>

## Why they give
<2-5 sourced sentences on motivation and fit: board years and tenure; foundation
mission and grant pattern by cause; their own public statements, donor stories,
important people and interests. Use the strongest evidence, with a record and
date for each claim. Label inference; if unknown, say so.>
**How we fit:** <1-2 sentences linking that pattern to our cause. Cite the basis;
label a proposed fit as inference. If our cause is not known, ask.>

## Giving to our organization
| Donor or vehicle | Outright gifts | Outstanding commitments | Total |
|---|---|---|---|

<Designations with year ranges; largest gift and year; pattern. Open pledges and
active proposals with amount, purpose, manager, date.>

## Relationship to our organization
<Degrees, volunteer and board roles, awards from us, event attendance, relatives
who are alumni. From the organization's records and public sources.>

## Contact history
<Chronological, most recent first, from contact reports. Date, who met, what was
said, what was agreed, what was declined. Attribute each entry to the officer
who reported it. If not provided: "Not provided. Ask <officer>."

## Relationships and warm paths
| Person | Connection | Their tie to us | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

## Recent events (last 24 months)
- <YYYY-MM-DD>: <event> [n]

## What we do not know
| Unknown | Why it matters | What would settle it |
|---|---|---|

## Sources
1. <Record or publication>, <date>. <URL>
```

Notes:

- Objectives, the ask and the contact history belong to the officer. Draft
  them from what the user told you and mark them "proposed; confirm with
  <officer>". Never invent a contact, a pledge or an objective.
- Name the record and date inside each sentence as well as in the Sources list.
- A **transition briefing** (a new president inheriting the relationship) uses
  the same template. Drop the Meeting table and Objectives; expand Contact
  history to the whole relationship, and add a line "Relationship owner and
  history: <who has carried the relationship, and since when>".
- Keep the whole document to six pages. Cut affiliations and wealth detail
  before you cut strategy, giving, or contact history. Keep "Why they give"
  brief; do not repeat the biography or fill gaps with guesses.
